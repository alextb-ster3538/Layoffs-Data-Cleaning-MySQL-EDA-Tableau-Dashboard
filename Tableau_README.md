📊 Tableau Dashboard — Global Layoff Trends (2020–2023)
Designed a clean, multi‑visual Tableau dashboard to highlight global layoff patterns across four years. The dashboard visualizes total layoffs, YoY changes, and geographic concentration using a consistent layout, clear spacing, and a narrative‑driven design.

<img width="1188" height="897" alt="Layoffs dashboard_tableau" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/03becf20-60bd-4d00-b64b-9108d0821ccd" />


Dashboard Highlights
- Total layoffs by year (2020–2023)
- Year‑over‑year percentage change
- Geographic distribution of layoffs

### 🔎 **Key Insights:** 

### **1️⃣ Total Layoffs by Year**
**Insight:**  
- Layoffs surged sharply in **2022**, marking the peak of global workforce reductions.  
- The trend reflects post‑pandemic market corrections and tech sector restructuring.  
- **2023** shows a moderate decline, suggesting stabilization and selective rehiring.  
- Earlier years (2020–2021) had smaller totals, consistent with early pandemic uncertainty rather than mass downsizing.

**Narrative Tip:**  
> “The spike in 2022 highlights the industry’s adjustment phase following rapid pandemic‑era expansion.”

---

### **2️⃣ Year‑over‑Year (YoY) Change**
**Insight:**  
- The **YoY % change** from 2021 → 2022 is the largest positive jump, confirming the layoff wave’s intensity.  
- **2023** records a **negative YoY change**, indicating contraction in layoff activity.  
- This reversal suggests companies began stabilizing headcount and focusing on efficiency rather than mass cuts.

**Narrative Tip:**  
> “After a record surge in 2022, layoffs declined YoY in 2023 — signaling early recovery and operational recalibration.”

---

### **3️⃣ Total Layoffs by Country (Map)**
**Insight:**  
- The **United States** dominates global layoffs, accounting for the majority of total counts.  
- **India, the UK, and Canada** follow, reflecting the global reach of tech and startup downsizing.  
- Smaller clusters appear in **Germany, Brazil, and Singapore**, showing regional ripple effects.  
- The map emphasizes that layoffs were **concentrated in tech‑heavy economies** rather than evenly distributed worldwide.

**Narrative Tip:**  
> “Layoffs were geographically concentrated — the U.S. led by a wide margin, underscoring its central role in global tech employment.”
